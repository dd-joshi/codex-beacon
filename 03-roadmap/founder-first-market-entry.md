# Founder-first market-entry plan

**Decision date:** 13 August 2026  
**Planning status:** **Superseded on 14 August 2026. Do not execute.** Retained as a future international-scale study. Current work is governed by the [solo-maker Lite/Pro plan](solo-maker-lite-pro-plan.md).  
**Founder assumption:** One founder is Product/Program owner until a role is deliberately delegated

> The current architecture also supersedes this study's original wired assumptions: Peri6 now uses authenticated same-private-LAN communication, exactly one active desktop pack, and a pack-agnostic generic ESP renderer under the [software prototype plan](software-prototype-plan.md). Battery and power-system engineering remain outside this repository and require separate readiness evidence.

## The decision

Peri6 will be **developed and tested from India, but validated and launched commercially in the United States first**.

This is not a bet that “Indian people will not buy it.” Some Indian creators will. It is a recognition that Peri6 is a narrow premium tool everywhere, and an approximately ₹9,000–₹12,000 discretionary desk device should not depend on Indian mass-market volume.

The working market sequence is:

1. United States — demand validation and first commercial pilot
2. United Kingdom — only after US field quality and support are stable
3. Selected European Union countries — only after market-specific compliance and service work
4. India — limited research/beta sales first; no volume forecast until paid local evidence exists

## The first customer—not “everyone with a PC”

The first gate cohort is:

> US-based independent video editors, motion designers, and 3D artists who use Windows, have a dedicated workstation/desk, and run at least three renders or exports per week that each take 15 minutes or longer.

Their first promised outcome is:

> **Start a render, walk away, and know when it finishes, fails, stalls, or needs you.**

Developers running builds and tests remain a secondary segment. They must not be mixed into the initial paid test because a blended audience can hide which job actually creates demand.

## The first 90 days

### Days 1–5 — prepare the test

Deliver:

- one-page recruiting screener using the exact cohort above;
- interview and recent-incident script;
- a 15-second storyboard showing Calm, Working, Done, and Attention;
- two messages to test: “Know when your render is done” and “See whether your computer is okay”;
- participant consent, evidence sheet, and refund procedure;
- a short consultation with an Indian chartered accountant/payment specialist before accepting foreign deposits.

Do not begin custom electronics, tooling, incorporation abroad, or paid brand work.

### Weeks 1–2 — prove the problem

Interview 20 qualified US creators. Interview five Indian creators separately for workflow and usability comparison; do not blend the results.

Pass this checkpoint only if:

- at least 12 of 20 US participants repeatedly leave or check long tasks;
- at least eight describe a recent missed completion, failed job, unnecessary wait, or equivalent consequence;
- render/export completion ranks above generic PC-health monitoring;
- current workarounds and the reason they fail are documented.

If this fails, stop recruiting for later tests and change the segment or problem before spending more.

### Weeks 2–4 — prove comprehension and message

Build a high-fidelity visual simulation and three to five crude physical desk rigs. Polish only what is necessary to represent the promised experience honestly.

Test Calm, Working, Done, Failed/Unknown, and Attention before explanation and after a one-minute introduction. Test at desk and across-room distance, in day and night conditions, with hue removed and motion reduced.

Pass only if:

- at least 80% understand the essential journey without instruction;
- at least 90% distinguish the core states after a one-minute introduction;
- failed, cancelled, unknown, and successful completion are never intentionally collapsed;
- the task-completion message materially outperforms generic health monitoring;
- fewer than 10% still describe the demonstrated value as only “RGB decoration.”

### Weeks 3–6 — prove repeated use

Run 10–15 two-week concierge desk trials. Use simulations, locally assembled rigs, or manual event control where needed; clearly disclose what is simulated. The purpose is behavior, not scalable engineering.

Pass only if:

- at least 65% repeatedly use the watched-task behavior;
- at least 60% use it on four or more days in each trial week;
- at least 40% say they would be “very disappointed” to lose it after removal;
- participants actually walk away, rather than merely saying the idea is attractive;
- no supported observed workflow shows a failed or cancelled task as Done.

### Weeks 4–7 — prove money, not compliments

Show the expected product, supported workflow, honest limitations, price, estimated delivery window, shipping, and tax treatment. Randomly assign qualified prospects to $99, $119, and $129 offers. Request a fully refundable USD $10 reservation only through a legally and operationally reviewed payment/refund path.

The reservation policy initiates an approved refund within five business days and states that bank/processor settlement may take longer. The public smoke test target is at least 500 qualified US visits. Pass only if:

- at least 30 visitors place a refundable reservation;
- at least 10 reservations occur in the $119 or $129 conditions;
- fewer than 30% of reservations come from friends, family, or personal contacts;
- cancellation after full price, delivery, and limitation disclosure stays below 15%;
- reservation money is ring-fenced and is not used to finance unresolved R&D.

A waitlist, survey answer, like, or “I would buy this” does not pass the money test. If compliant payment collection is not ready, the decision is **Hold**, not Go.

### Week 8 — Gate G1

Proceed only when the problem, comprehension, repeated-use, trust, and paid-reservation thresholds all pass. Record one of four outcomes:

- **Go:** authorize Alpha planning and the next capped budget.
- **Conditional Go:** only a bounded, non-critical issue remains with an owner, cost, deadline, and objective retest.
- **Hold:** evidence is incomplete; no new engineering commitment.
- **Stop/Pivot:** the physical product, audience, price, or promise is unsupported.

### Weeks 9–12 — only after a Go

- select the first two named render/export workflows;
- write Alpha requirements and the pack-host lifecycle, generic scene, capability, authentication, and device-event contracts;
- obtain contractor statements of work for Windows, embedded/electrical, and industrial/optical design;
- obtain preliminary US compliance, customs classification, landed-cost, and 3PL quotes;
- choose an off-the-shelf enclosure path versus custom enclosure with explicit cost/schedule consequences;
- decide the legal/payment structure with qualified Indian and US advisers;
- approve no more than the cumulative Alpha cap shown below.

## Initial ₹3 lakh validation ceiling

Founder time and refundable customer deposits are excluded. These are maximum planning allowances, not spending targets.

| Use | Cap |
|---|---:|
| Qualified US recruiting, interview/diary/trial incentives | ₹85,000 |
| Visual demonstration, landing page, and test assets | ₹35,000 |
| Three to five crude rigs, local fabrication, and participant shipping | ₹70,000 |
| Qualified traffic and community/recruiting experiments | ₹50,000 |
| Indian CA, payment, export, and preliminary legal consultation | ₹25,000 |
| Research tools, refunds, failures, and contingency | ₹35,000 |
| **Maximum** | **₹3,00,000** |

Release the money in two steps:

1. Approve at most ₹80,000 for problem interviews and the minimum visual stimulus.
2. Release the remaining amount only after the problem checkpoint passes.

No part of this ceiling is authorized for production PCB design, enclosure tooling, certification, inventory, foreign incorporation, full-time hiring, or a crowdfunding launch.

## Stage-funded path after demand proof

The figures below are directional 2026 planning ranges, not supplier quotations. They exclude founder salary and can increase materially if the founder cannot cover product, software, or technical coordination.

| Stage | Incremental cash range | Evidence that unlocks it |
|---|---:|---|
| Discovery and low-fidelity validation | Up to ₹3L | Approved now in two releases |
| Integrated Alpha | ₹3L–₹7L | Full G1 pass: paid demand plus repeated use |
| EVT | ₹8L–₹18L | Trusted Alpha, bounded architecture, viable early cost |
| DVT and tooling | ₹20L–₹45L | EVT reliability, pre-compliance, DFM, and no false-Done blocker |
| PVT/readiness | ₹15L–₹35L | Production design, formal compliance, field beta, and downside economics pass |
| First saleable batch, maximum 250 | ₹12L–₹25L | PVT yield, exact landed cost, funded warranty/returns, and cash contingency |

A serious 250-unit launch therefore has a rough cumulative cash range of approximately **₹61 lakh to ₹1.33 crore**, excluding founder salary. Treat this as a financing boundary, not a promise to spend. A 1,000-unit first order is explicitly rejected; reorder only after the canary and real sell-through.

The eight-week sprint assumes at least 15–20 focused founder hours per week, with approximately half spent speaking with, recruiting, or supporting participants. If that time is unavailable, extend the calendar; do not cut the evidence thresholds.

## Founder and team model

Until G1, the founder personally owns:

- customer interviews and synthesis;
- product promise, priorities, and stop/pivot decisions;
- experiment integrity and the cash ledger;
- basic simulations/rig coordination;
- vendor discovery and evidence filing.

Use specialists only when evidence unlocks their work:

| Timing | Contract capability | Why it is not a pre-G1 hire |
|---|---|---|
| After G1 | Windows application/installer engineer | Demand should exist before production software |
| After G1 | Embedded/electrical engineer | Architecture should serve a validated workflow |
| After G1 | Industrial/optical designer | Premium form is costly and can disguise weak value |
| EVT onward | DFM, sourcing, quality, and compliance specialists | Exact design and market determine the work |
| DVT onward | Manufacturing partner and US fulfillment | Needed only for a verified, frozen product |

Avoid fixed hiring before Alpha/EVT. Contracts must assign source code, CAD, BOM, fixtures, test limits, signing keys, and manufacturing files to the company.

## Cross-border operating plan

- Build prototypes and manage engineering from India.
- Use a compliant, adviser-reviewed route for foreign reservations and refunds.
- Do not form a US entity merely to appear global. Decide only after paid evidence and advice on Indian ODI/tax and US tax/operating obligations.
- Kickstarter is a later launch amplifier, not a discovery tool. India is not currently a directly supported creator country, so an eligible structure would be required.
- Budget roughly 8%–10% for Kickstarter/platform processing before advertising, tax, freight, returns, and support if that channel is eventually chosen.
- Do not plan commercial one-parcel-at-a-time India-to-US fulfillment. Obtain the exact HS classification and duty treatment, then batch-import to one US 3PL/import setup after the pilot is funded.
- Defer the UK and EU until US field quality is stable. EU consumer, responsible-person, WEEE, product-safety, and cybersecurity work is a separate expansion program.

| Trigger | Entity/payment decision |
|---|---|
| Before any foreign reservation | Indian CA/payment advice, written refund path, and required invoice/export records |
| G1 fails | Refund promptly; do not form a foreign entity |
| G1 passes | Compare an India-based export route with a US entity using written Indian ODI/tax and US tax/operating advice |
| Kickstarter becomes the chosen launch channel | Establish an eligible structure only after a working Alpha and prelaunch demand proof |
| Before commercial orders | Finalize seller of record, importer of record, duty/tax, US 3PL, returns, insurance, and customer terms |

## Stop and pivot rules

| Evidence | Decision |
|---|---|
| Problem is not weekly for the chosen cohort | Change segment or stop |
| Task completion is loved but Health is not | Narrow the product and positioning to trusted task status |
| A physical signal is not better than existing notification behavior | Stop custom hardware; test a software-only solution |
| Interest is strong but money fails at viable price | Simplify enclosure/scope and rerun the paid test; do not subsidize demand |
| Paid intent passes but repeated use fails | Do not build; novelty is not retention |
| Any repeated false-Done event occurs | Hold the workflow until detection can be made trustworthy |
| Fully landed US cost cannot plausibly stay near or below $42 at $119, or downside contribution is negative | Redesign, reprice, or stop before tooling |

## What happens next

The immediate dependency order is:

1. Founder accepts Product/Program accountability and the ₹80,000 first release.
2. Recruit the first 20 US creators using the exact screen.
3. Conduct interviews before polishing the object.
4. Build only the visual/physical evidence needed for comprehension and desk trials.
5. Complete adviser review before taking foreign deposits.
6. Hold Gate G1 in week 8 and stop, pivot, hold, or fund Alpha.
