# Instagram build-in-public plan

**Decision date:** 14 August 2026  
**Status:** Current acquisition and journey-record plan  
**Purpose:** Turn genuine product work into trust, qualified conversations, and paid Lite/Pro evidence without creating a second full-time job

> Technical content follows the [local Wi-Fi software prototype plan](../03-roadmap/software-prototype-plan.md): PC and Peri6 share a private home LAN, exactly one desktop pack is active, and the unchanged ESP renders only generic scenes. Battery and power-system development and claims are outside the current repository and content scope.

## What Instagram is—and is not

Instagram is useful for:

- making an unknown solo engineer visible and believable;
- demonstrating a physical product faster than explaining it;
- recording decisions, failures, improvements, and early customer proof;
- discovering which problem/category earns attention;
- sending qualified people to one Lite/Pro offer page.

It is not proof merely because a Reel receives views, likes, votes, or followers. Paid reservations, activation, retained use, referrals, refunds, and contribution remain the business evidence.

Do not expect Instagram creator payouts. The commercial goal is selling Peri6, not becoming an influencer.

## Account position

Use a separate public Creator account after the employment/IP gate passes. It may use the working product name, but the biography should remain personally truthful:

> One electronics engineer in India building a calm, local-first status light after work. Real prototypes, failures, costs, and five-unit batches.

Feed language: simple English for broad usefulness. Stories may use natural Hinglish when it improves connection. A face is optional; hands, voice, and real work are enough, but the account must clearly belong to a real maker rather than pretending to be a large company.

Pin only three posts:

1. the 20-second human problem and hero demonstration;
2. exact Lite versus Pro capabilities, prices, and current Published packs;
3. current build/test/batch status.

Suggested Highlights: `Demo`, `Build`, `Tests`, `Packs`, `Lite-Pro`, `FAQ`.

## How to work with Instagram, not chase it

Instagram does not have one permanent trick or algorithm. Use current in-product guidance and measure actual behavior:

- Instagram's [Best Practices area in the Professional Dashboard](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/) provides updated and personalized creation, engagement, reach, monetization, and guideline advice.
- [Trial Reels](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/) can test a format with non-followers first and expose view/share comparisons after roughly 24 hours; use them when available.
- Instagram provides Replays and retention-chart insights to diagnose where viewers leave a Reel. [Meta's Reels insights update](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/new-ways-to-create-content-on-instagram/)
- Film original build footage. Do not repost other creators, remove/crop other-platform watermarks, buy engagement, or manufacture fake activity.

Optimize for what a human would watch, save, send, ask about, or buy—not for an alleged secret hack.

## Content time budget

Maximum **three hours per week** and no more than 20% of total side-project time.

Base cadence:

- Wednesday: one 15–30 second Reel from real work;
- Sunday: one evidence-rich carousel;
- Stories: at most two three-frame sets during genuine build/test sessions;
- no compulsory daily post, Live schedule, or paid advertising.

If content exceeds three hours for two consecutive weeks, reduce to one weekly evidence post plus natural Stories.

## Five-minute capture protocol

During every real work session, capture only:

1. one five-second `before/problem` clip;
2. one five-second `work/test` clip;
3. one five-second `result/failure` clip;
4. one photograph of the test card, BOM, or decision;
5. one 20-second voice note: what changed, what the number was, and what happens next.

Do not edit during engineering time. Batch-edit the week's Reel and carousel in 60–90 minutes on Sunday. The content archive is therefore generated by the work rather than competing with it.

## Content pillars

| Pillar | What people receive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product moment | An immediately understandable outcome | Start Blender render, leave, persistent Done marker |
| Build transformation | A satisfying visible change | Exposed LEDs to six isolated diffused cells |
| Engineering evidence | A useful test or trustworthy number | Failed render must never produce green; 20-cycle result |
| Honest decision | A trade-off and its measured consequence | Cheap diffuser versus better isolation; why one lost |
| Category proof | A real workflow with exactly one active desktop pack | OBS recording state or explicit developer command result |
| Architecture proof | Desktop pack/pattern changes while compatible ESP firmware stays unchanged | Focus and Blender produce distinct scenes through the same generic renderer |
| Maker/business receipt | Transparency without fake hustle | BOM, build time, refund count, Lite/Pro selection, batch gate |
| Customer proof | Consent-based real use | Numbered unit test, safe packing, desk use, referral |

Avoid generic motivation, random Arduino tutorials, decorative RGB clips with no meaning, fake office/company imagery, unrelated memes, and features that are not genuinely being built.

## Repeatable Reel structures

### 1. Problem → release

Hook: “This render takes 34 minutes, and I keep checking it.”  
Visual: start → walk away → persistent result light.  
CTA: “See the exact supported workflow and price in the pinned post.”

### 2. Failure receipt

Hook: “The green light lied once. That makes the product useless.”  
Visual: reproduce → one-line cause → corrected counter/test.  
CTA: “Would you trust Done only after this test?”

### 3. Build delta

Hook: “Yesterday: six bright dots. Today: six readable cells.”  
Visual: before → two build clips → across-room after.  
CTA: one specific comparison question.

### 4. Category proof

Hook: “Same hardware, different job: is OBS actually recording?”  
Visual: application event → physical state → stop/error.  
CTA: “Choose Creator, Stream, Developer, or Health on the product page.”

### 5. Premium trade-off

Hook: “This ₹X part costs more. Here is the visible reason.”  
Visual: side-by-side → measurement → decision.  
CTA: “Lite and Pro use the same hardware quality; Pro pays for capability breadth.”

### 6. Batch receipt

Hook: “Unit 04 took 2h 18m. Here is what passed.”  
Visual: assembly → end-of-line card → serial/pack with private data hidden.  
CTA: current truthful batch status.

Show the product result within the first seconds. Use readable on-screen captions because many people will not begin with sound. Cut dead time; preserve enough context to make the test credible.

## Repeatable carousel structures

- **Decision log:** result-first cover → constraint → option A/B → method → numbers → decision → next gate.
- **Test report:** claim → setup/sample → pass limit → result → failure → correction → retest.
- **BOM receipt:** part groups → actual unit cost → build/test time → reserve → price/contribution → exclusions.
- **Lite/Pro:** same hardware/core → exactly one active pack in either tier → selectable-pack breadth and other exact differences → current Published packs → full price/reservation/refund → real slots.
- **Monthly report:** personal hours → cash spent → qualified visits → reservations → units → active users → defects/refunds → contribution → continue/hold/stop.

Caption formula: one-line problem, two measured facts, one decision, one CTA. One post gets one CTA.

## Thirteen-week story arc

| Week | Engineering/customer event | Reel | Carousel/CTA |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Employment/IP gate and problem definition | Why repeated checking wastes attention | Personal constraints and rules; no sale CTA |
| 2 | Generic ESP renderer proof | Working → Done → Failed from generic keyframes | Why the ESP knows no application or pack |
| 3 | Optics/diffuser iteration | Before/after isolation across a room | Test method and rejected option |
| 4 | Focus and Blender packs on one host | Switch packs; start render, walk away, Done marker | Distinct pack visuals on unchanged firmware |
| 5 | Software-prototype gate | Same hardware, one active pack, upgradeable desktop | Exact current capability; no paid slots until excluded hardware dependencies clear |
| 6 | Failure and disconnect testing | Router loss/rejoin, stale-scene expiry, and a real false-state correction | Raw test counter and current gate status |
| 7 | Next-pack selection | OBS versus Developer proof | Paid selections decide; poll is only input |
| 8 | Five-unit build only if software, paid-demand, and external hardware-readiness gates pass | One numbered unit from parts to pass | Build time, BOM, EOL checklist |
| 9 | First setup/activation | What confused the first external user | Support minutes and correction |
| 10 | Next category proof | Real Stream or Developer workflow | Supported/unsupported matrix |
| 11 | System Health feasibility | Why high CPU is not automatically danger | Safe thresholds, unknown data, forbidden claims |
| 12 | Customer/retention evidence | Consented desk moment or honest absence | Active use, refunds, referrals, contribution |
| 13 | Ninety-day verdict | What actually worked and failed | Continue, narrow, one-tier, software pivot, or stop |

If a scheduled engineering event did not happen, publish a short honest gate update or nothing. Never invent work to maintain the calendar.

## Conversion funnel

```text
Original evidence post
        ↓
Pinned human demo
        ↓
One profile link
        ↓
60-second product page
        ↓
Choose Lite or Pro
        ↓
Refundable reservation
        ↓
Build-to-order unit
        ↓
Retained use / referral
```

Orders do not live only in DMs. DMs answer questions; one page records source, current capabilities, final price, slot count, estimated delivery, shipping, handmade status, limitations, and refund terms. All scarcity is factual.

CTA ladder:

- Weeks 1–3: “Which exact task do you repeatedly check?” or view the pinned demo.
- Weeks 4–5: view current capabilities and select a category/tier.
- Weeks 5–8: reserve one of five real maker slots.
- After delivery: join the next batch only if the first quality/use gate passes.

## Scoreboard

Record raw counts weekly because early percentages can be misleading.

### Inputs

- product hours;
- content hours;
- original work sessions captured;
- Reel/carousel delivered.

### Content diagnostics

- non-follower reach;
- average watch time/retention and Replays when available;
- shares and saves;
- qualified workflow comments/DMs;
- profile visits and link clicks.

### Commercial outcomes

- landing visits attributable to Instagram;
- category selections;
- Lite and Pro selections;
- reservation starts/completions/refunds;
- purchases, activations, retained use, referrals, and contribution.

Followers and likes are context, not goals.

## Channel gates and stop rules

### Day 30

- at least eight evidence posts total;
- at least 100 qualified landing visits across all sources;
- at least 10 explicit category/tier selections;
- at least five unrelated reservations, including two Pro;
- at least one reservation attributable to Instagram.

If Instagram produces fewer than 20 qualified link clicks after eight strong evidence posts, stop treating it as the primary channel. Continue testing through targeted creator/developer communities.

If at least 100 qualified product-page visits produce fewer than five reservations, the offer/value/trust has failed. More Reels are not the remedy.

### Day 60

- five external users or paid orders;
- at least four successful activations;
- zero false Done for failed/cancelled tested workflows;
- no more than one refund;
- content remains within three hours weekly.

Deposits with poor activation or retained use indicate a product problem, not a content problem; pause selling.

### Day 90

- at least 10 unrelated reservations/purchases;
- at least three users retained weekly after 30 days;
- at least two organic/referral orders;
- at least one Instagram-sourced customer;
- refund/defect and contribution remain within the [product plan](../03-roadmap/solo-maker-lite-pro-plan.md).

No paid advertisements, giveaways, follow-for-follow, bought followers, or paid creator collaborations before 10 organic customers and positive unit contribution.

## Privacy, employer, and customer safety

Before posting every asset, check for:

- employer screen, device, logo, badge, email, calendar, code, lab, document, supplier, or location;
- home address, shipping label, phone number, customer DM, serial/licence key, Wi-Fi details, or live location;
- protectable design detail, security/update mechanism, secret, signing key, or credential;
- a person or customer desk without explicit publication consent;
- a claim that exceeds the exact tested configuration.

Do not publish battery-life, charging, portability, power-safety, or power-development claims. Any incidental prototype power setup shown in footage is not a designed or validated product subsystem.

Record the journey from personal space and personal time. The maker story is valuable only while it remains truthful and safe.
