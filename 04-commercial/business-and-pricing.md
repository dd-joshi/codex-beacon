# Business, positioning, and pricing plan

> **Status:** Future scaled/international commercial reference. Current Indian maker pricing and Lite/Pro economics are governed by the [solo-maker plan](../03-roadmap/solo-maker-lite-pro-plan.md), and technical architecture by the [software prototype plan](../03-roadmap/software-prototype-plan.md). Battery and power-system engineering are excluded from this repository; these future economics cannot authorize a paid unit until a separate complete-product cost and readiness input exists.

## Category position

Peri6 should own a narrow category:

> **A calm desktop status instrument that tells you whether your workstation is okay, working, finished, or needs attention.**

Adjacent products typically emphasize one of four things:

- miniature feeds and information screens;
- active shortcut/control surfaces;
- single-bit busy/availability lights;
- detailed PC sensor dashboards and decorative RGB.

Peri6 occupies the space between them: passive rather than demanding, interpreted rather than data-heavy, useful alone rather than purely social, and tasteful rather than decorative-first.

## Positioning hierarchy

### Emotional benefit

**Walk away without wondering.**

### Functional promise

Peri6 shows when important computer work is running, finished, failed, stalled, or genuinely needs attention.

### Product proof

- Persistent completion memory
- Honest working versus trusted progress
- Interpreted sustained conditions instead of raw spikes
- One-action explanation
- Local, account-free operation
- Calm, accessible six-cell language

### Specification

Local-only same-home-router communication between an upgradeable Windows companion and a pack-agnostic ESP six-cell renderer, with six isolated light cells, optional ambient-light adaptation, and one physical control. USB, if present, is for development/recovery rather than normal product data.

Multiple Published desktop packs may be installed, but exactly one is active at a time. Each pack owns its application integration and visual identity; the device receives only generic scenes, so compatible new pack patterns do not require routine ESP firmware changes.

Specifications come last in marketing. The product sells recovered attention, not LED count.

## Working messages to test

1. **Know when your work is finished.**
2. **Your computer has body language.**
3. **The physical status bar your computer forgot.**
4. **It stays quiet when everything is fine—and remembers what finished while you were away.**

The lead line is chosen by qualified reservation conversion and accurate 15-second comprehension, not internal preference.

## Primary buyer

The initial paid-evidence buyer is a US-based independent video editor, motion designer, or 3D artist using Windows and running at least three renders/exports per week that last 15 minutes or longer.

Additional buyer characteristics:

- creator or technical power user;
- runs long tasks several times per week;
- values premium desk tools;
- currently checks, waits, uses remote access, or depends on unreliable notifications;
- prefers local, low-distraction tools;
- comfortable with a $99–$129 considered accessory purchase.

The user and buyer are initially the same person. Developers are a secondary validation cohort, not blended into the initial price result. Team/enterprise purchasing is a later motion with different deployment, admin, compliance, and support requirements.

## Purchase reasons

- Leave renders/builds/backups without repeated checking
- Notice completion from across the room
- Understand whether high activity is productive or problematic
- Avoid overlays and another permanent screen
- Own a tasteful object that remains useful after novelty
- Use local processing without an account or subscription

## Likely objections

| Objection | Required answer |
|---|---|
| “My computer already sends notifications.” | Demonstrate persistent, room-readable completion and lower interruption cost |
| “It is just RGB.” | Show interpreted state, honest task outcomes, completion memory, and plain-language explanation |
| “Will it work with my app/GPU?” | Public compatibility checker and explicit generic/verified behavior |
| “I do not want another heavy RGB utility.” | Publish resource targets, local architecture, no account, no hidden suite |
| “Six lights are ambiguous.” | Minimal vocabulary, fixed position, accessible patterns, one-action explanation |
| “What if the app breaks after an update?” | Supported matrix, safe fallback, ongoing compatibility/security term |
| “Does Pro monitor every pack simultaneously?” | No. Both tiers run exactly one selected pack at a time; Pro expands the selectable Published pack library, history, settings, and automation |
| “Why is it worth over $100?” | Premium optics/build plus repeated recovered-attention use, not raw component count |

## V1 business model

- One hardware SKU
- One launch finish
- Direct-to-consumer in the United States first; India is the development/research base
- Only the entitled Published desktop packs are included; exactly one is active at a time
- No mandatory subscription
- Optional mounts or icon/accessory packs only after the core product is stable
- No retail margin dependency in V1 economics

Potential future paid cloud capability must cover a real ongoing cost and independent value—such as remote machine status or hosted CI connectors—without removing local V1 behavior.

## Pricing hypothesis

**Working MSRP:** USD $119  
**Research range:** $99–$129  
**Possible limited founders/early price:** $99–$109, only if contribution and price positioning remain healthy.

Price is validated through:

1. problem/value interviews;
2. structured price sensitivity testing;
3. message-specific landing tests;
4. refundable paid reservations;
5. retained willingness to keep/pay after multi-week use.

Email waitlists and positive interviews are not sufficient price evidence.

## Unit economics targets

At $119 MSRP:

- $30 landed COGS yields approximately 74.8% product gross margin before fulfillment.
- $36 landed COGS yields approximately 69.7% product gross margin before fulfillment.
- $42 landed COGS yields approximately 64.7% product gross margin before fulfillment.

Targets:

- Fully landed US cost for the 250-unit pilot: near or below $42, with an explicit exception above it
- Scaled landed COGS target after proven reorder volume: ≤$30
- DTC product gross margin before fulfillment: at least approximately 64% for the pilot and approximately 70% at scale
- Contribution margin after payment fees, pick/pack, outbound subsidy, returns, warranty, and support: at least 35% for the controlled pilot and 45% at steady-state scale
- Warranty/returns reserve: provisional 3–5% of revenue until field evidence improves the estimate
- NRE contingency: 15–20% until PVT
- Service stock: 20–30 units or approved 2–3%

Wholesale economics should still be modeled at roughly 50–55% of MSRP even though retail is deferred. If the product can never support channel margin, that strategic limitation should be explicit.

The $99 founders price is a bounded acquisition/validation condition, not the basis for permanent economics. Model it separately with the same fully landed cost, service, return, and fulfillment assumptions.

## Landed cost model

COGS must include:

- PCBA components and fabrication;
- controller/radio interface, sensor, control, and LEDs within the approved product scope;
- optical light engine/diffusers;
- enclosure, base, mount, feet, and hardware;
- assembly labor;
- provisioning, calibration, and factory test;
- expected scrap and controlled rework;
- packaging and printed material;
- inbound freight, duties, and landed handling.

Track separately:

- industrial design and engineering labor;
- tooling/NRE and fixture development;
- compliance lab and registrations;
- legal/IP and product-liability insurance;
- software signing/distribution and security review;
- 3PL setup and support tools;
- launch content/review seeding;
- returns processing and replacement freight;
- working capital and financing costs.

## Required financial scenarios

### Base

Expected MSRP, planned COGS, forecast returns/support, and planned first-run sell-through.

### Downside

- Landed COGS +15%
- 20% promotional discount on part of inventory
- 8% total returns
- Higher support/replacement freight
- Slower sell-through and longer cash cycle

### Severe downside

- Production delay and partial rework
- Demand materially below reservations
- Freight/duty shock
- Common software compatibility issue
- Additional compliance or tooling correction

The program cannot place the production order unless the downside case remains contribution-positive and the severe-downside cash exposure is survivable without immediate sell-through.

## Funding and cash requirements

Before Gate G1, spending is capped at ₹3 lakh under the [founder-first market-entry plan](../03-roadmap/founder-first-market-entry.md). No tooling, inventory, foreign incorporation, or fixed hiring is authorized by this commercial model.

Before mass production, the company must be able to fund:

- factory deposit and final balance;
- tooling/fixture balances;
- compliance and registrations;
- inbound freight, duty, tax, and importer costs;
- fulfillment setup and launch inventory storage;
- replacements and warranty reserve;
- return/refund timing;
- 15% minimum cash contingency after committed obligations.

Do not use customer preorders to conceal an unfunded product-development risk. If preorders are taken, disclose schedule, uncertainty, refund conditions, and what evidence has already passed.

## Name, brand, and IP

“Peri6” is a working name. Before public DVT promotion:

- conduct trademark clearance in applicable hardware/software classes;
- secure domain/handles after preliminary clearance;
- review final industrial design before public disclosure;
- assess design registration/protection where useful;
- perform focused freedom-to-operate review for novel mounting, optics, interaction, or progress detection;
- create a claims-to-evidence matrix.

Brand behavior rules:

- no rainbow startup;
- red only for sustained actionable conditions;
- unknown never shown as zero;
- failed/cancelled never shown as successful;
- calmness and honesty over spectacle.

## Expansion model

Expansion follows evidence, not feature excitement:

1. More verified creator adapters in the United States
2. United Kingdom market entry after US field stability
3. Selected European Union entry after a separate compliance/service gate
4. Limited India edition only after paid local evidence
5. macOS support after an equivalent reliability plan
6. Optional accessories, off-LAN remote/cloud/mobile operation, team capability, and hosted connectors through separate gates

Each expansion requires its own customer, economics, quality, support, privacy, and compliance gate.

## Commercial gate

Before tooling:

- target users repeatedly use and miss the product;
- at least one message communicates value accurately in 15 seconds;
- qualified refundable reservations exist near viable pricing;
- fewer than 10% of tested prospects reduce it to “just RGB” after the demonstration;
- target landed COGS remains plausible;
- downside contribution is positive;
- name/design risk is bounded.

Before production:

- exact landed cost and working-capital timing are known;
- launch price and discount floor are approved;
- warranty/returns/support costs are funded;
- claims match verified compatibility and behavior;
- first-run inventory can be held without existential cash pressure.
